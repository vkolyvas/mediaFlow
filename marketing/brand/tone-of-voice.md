# Tone of voice

Two audiences, one voice. The Greek and the English streams differ in
language and in what they know about Greece, not in personality.

## Voice principles

**Helpful before promotional.** The first two lines of a post should be
useful to someone even if they never click. A caption that only exists to sell
a link reads as an advert and is skipped.

**Specific beats impressive.** "Three questions to ask a photographer before
you book" beats "Find your perfect photographer". Concrete details are what
saves and shares.

**Calm, not urgent.** The couple is planning something that takes a year.
Fear tactics and countdown pressure are both dishonest and off-brand.

**We are a guide, not a critic.** We point at the directory and explain what
is in it. We do not tell a couple that one vendor is better than another
unless we have a defensible, current method for saying so.

## We say / we do not say

| We say | We do not say |
| --- | --- |
| "5 photographers to discover in Athens" | "The top 5 photographers in Athens" |
| "WeddingWise lists 214 photographers in Athens" | "The best photographers in Athens" |
| "A photographer on WeddingWise has a 4.8 rating from 32 reviews" | "Our favourite photographer" |
| "How to tell a real review from a fake one" | "Don't get scammed - trust only us" |
| "Start planning in Athens" | "Sign up now before it's too late" |
| "Vendors can claim their free profile" | "Over 4,000 couples already trust us" |

The middle column is not banned by grammar - it is banned because we have no
defensible method behind it, and because it damages the directory when it
turns out to be wrong.

## Claimed vs unclaimed

We distinguish them in the copy, always, when a vendor is named:

- **Claimed** - the vendor manages their own profile and is in the programme.
  "Elena K. manages her own profile on WeddingWise."
- **Unclaimed** - the listing exists because we imported public information.
  "Elena K. is listed on WeddingWise; she has not claimed her profile."

We never call an unclaimed listing a partner, a recommendation or a
discovery in the sense of an endorsement. We can absolutely show it as a
listing worth looking at.

## Greek voice

- Use a warm, natural Greek ("σας", "θα σας βοηθήσει"), not translated
  marketing English ("Η λύση σας για κάθε γάμο").
- Greek couples understand the local rules - the "μίσα μίσα" tax discussion,
  the paperwork at the Δήμος, that a κέρνος at the end of the evening is a
  different thing from a κέρνος at the start. These specifics are the whole
  reason the Greek stream exists.
- Greek is written in the Greek alphabet. Do not transliterate Greek venue or
  category names into Latin script in Greek copy.

## English voice

- The English stream is written for a couple who are planning a wedding in
  Greece and may not live here. Practical details about how things work
  locally are the differentiator - dates, logistics, what is customary.
- Use the Greek place names people actually search for in English (Santorini,
  Mykonos, Corfu) and keep the Greek spelling when it is the actual name of
  the venue or the category ("DJ", "florist" rather than "disc jockey").
- No Americanised assumptions. The couple may be in London, Berlin or Sydney.

## Mechanics

- Caption length: 150-400 characters for feed posts, up to 900 for a guide
  where the structure carries it.
- Emojis: at most one cluster, at the end, never in every post. This is a
  wedding brand, not a lifestyle influencer account.
- Hashtags: 5-8, specific, in the language of the caption. Never more than
  fifteen.
- Line breaks: one blank line between paragraphs. Instagram truncates at
  roughly 125 characters, so front-load the point.
- Numbers are specific: "214 photographers", "3 questions", "€" only when a
  real price exists in the data.
